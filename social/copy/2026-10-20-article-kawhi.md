Kawhi Leonard, at 34 years old, went from a good starter to the fifth-best player in the league per minute. His impact score, which measures how much a player helps per minute compared with an average NBA minute, went from +0.86 to +2.17. No player 34 or older has ever gained that much in one season, and he is the only star since 1978 to set career highs in usage and true shooting in the same season after 33.

Historically, only eight players have surged to star level after 31, and none of them kept three-quarters of that gain the next season; Steve Nash kept the most, 45%. Kawhi's surge came in two parts, a heavier role and hotter shooting, and those two parts age differently. The bigger role usually lasts. The hot shooting, a career-high 52% from mid-range per Cleaning the Glass, usually fades.
