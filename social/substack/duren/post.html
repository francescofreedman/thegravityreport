Abstract. Every other player in our top ten last season took at least 22% of his shots from three. Jalen Duren took none of them, and our per-minute rating still put him as the 7th best player in the league. That hasn't happened in over a decade. From 2015 to 2025, there wasn't a single player who never shot threes that finished in the top ten. Back in the 2004–05 season there were four of them, including the best player in the league. Today we look at how that kind of player has disappeared, how Duren is a top player without having a skill that is more important than ever in the modern game, and why we rank him 14th. Duren and the Pistons were in a four-month-long contract standoff, but now that it's finally over and he's getting paid $200 million over the next five years, we give it an 80% chance that he finishes top 15 next July.

What's in the article

  • The odd one out: the ten best players per minute last season all took at least 22% of their shots from three, except Duren, who took none (GRAVITY Historical, 1,500+ minutes).
  • Ten years without one: in 2004–05 four of the top ten were pure non-shooters, Kevin Garnett first among them. From 2015–16 through 2024–25 the count was zero.
  • How he does it: .688 true shooting on a 23.6% usage rate, 20.9% of available rebounds, a free throw for every two shots. First in efficiency under load among the 164 players with 1,500 minutes.
  • Why we rank him 14th: the playoffs. His playoff BPM of −1.7 against Cleveland and Orlando cost him 4.3 points of gravity score, the only player in The 644 at the floor. Without it he would rank 8th.
