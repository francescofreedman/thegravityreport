The method

How TORQUE works

  1. 1Every player on the roster is priced. Veterans carry their GRAVITY projection. Rookies and players back from abroad have no NBA record, so they start at replacement level (what a team can sign for nothing) plus a small bonus for youth.
  2. 2Minutes are handed out like a real rotation: the best players get the most, the 13th man a few. Players who missed games last year are expected to miss some again, but not all of them.
  3. 3A team plays 240 minutes a night. If the roster's expected minutes run over, everyone shares the cut; any minutes left empty are filled at replacement level.
  4. 4A small bonus goes to teams whose value sits in their top three players: stars decide more games than depth.

Why we changed the team model

The July edition ranked teams by adding up GRAVITY projections, weighted by last season's minutes. It ignored rookies and returnees, and it squeezed every player's minutes by the same factor when a roster was deep, so signing bench players could lower a team's number. We built and tested 17 team models on 49 seasons of data: every constant was set on 1981–2010, then each model was scored on 2011–2026, seasons it was never tuned on, and checked by independent reviewers. TORQUE won. Blending in a team's own results from last season (margin, playoffs, luck) made every model worse on the unseen seasons: once the roster is priced, last season's record adds nothing.
