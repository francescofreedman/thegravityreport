2 · Two-year blend

2025-26 carries 75% weight per minute played, 2024-25 carries 25% — so a star who lost 2025-26 to injury is judged mostly on his last healthy year, and a breakout season mostly on itself. Below ~2,200 total minutes the rating shrinks toward replacement level (−1.9z). The 2024-25 term also carries a 10% CTG on/off component.

4 · Rookies

Draft slot sets a prior mapped onto the veteran score distribution (pick 1 ≈ 84th percentile of the league — history says even elite rookies rarely crack the top 75 in year one). College production (BPM, TS%, usage, WS/40 from Sports-Reference) then moves each player up to ±8 percentile points. That's how Cameron Boozer jumps his slot and Dailyn Swain falls below his.

The calls the model makes where this ranking departs from the narrative — and why

№ 1 · The quiet answer

Jokić over the MVP

Voters gave SGA back-to-back MVPs. The data gave Jokić the league lead in BPM (14.2), VORP (9.2) and Win Shares (14.9) at .670 true shooting — one of the best statistical seasons ever recorded. SGA (#2, 92.9) is close; it just was never a coin flip.

№ 4 · Per-minute truth

Giannis on 36 games

Two calf strains and a knee bruise limited him to 36 games — but at 27.6/9.8/5.4 with a 9.5 BPM and +14.8 filtered on/off, the per-minute player is untouched. The availability discount is priced in and he still lands #4. Miami just traded half its future for the right ceiling.

№ 6 · The resurrection

Kawhi, age 34, top six

62 games, 8.0 BPM, +14.4 filtered on/off, then 27.9 PER playoff basketball. The model doesn't remember 2022-24 — it sees the second-best two-way wing season in the sample and ranks it. Toronto's Ingram-plus-picks bet suddenly reads differently.

№ 14 · The believer call

Jalen Duren's leap is real

Age-22 season: .688 TS on 23.6% usage, 11.0 Win Shares, 5.0 BPM as Detroit's anchor. Every input the model trusts — efficiency at volume, youth, on/off — says this was a genuine ascension year, not a stat mirage. Boldest top-15 placement on the board.

№ 11 vs № 22 · The swap

LaMelo over Edwards

The season nobody wanted to see: LaMelo's 4.7 BPM, +9.9 filtered on/off (finally healthy, 72 games) against Ant's 4.5 BPM and +0.1 filtered on/off. Two-year track keeps it close; the model refuses to overrule its own inputs. Minnesota now employs both — the trade that decides who was right.

№ 17 · June ≠ April

Brunson: champion, not top-10

Finals MVP, a 45-point closeout, the first Knicks title since 1973 — and an 82-game record of 3.1 BPM, +1.3 filtered on/off behind a deep bench. The playoff term nudges him up, not into the top 10. This is a ranking of the whole season, not of June.

№ 24 · The discount

Tatum's 16-game audit

Back March 6 from the Achilles: 4.8 BPM in 522 minutes — nearly his old self. The blend leans on his 2024-25 All-NBA season, applies a modest re-rupture-era discount, and lands him #20 with the arrow pointing up.

№ 84 vs № 88 · Production over hype

Boozer above Dybantsa

Dybantsa went #1 and earned it on talent. But Boozer's Duke season — 22.5/10.2, .653 TS, 18.7 BPM, .327 WS/40 — is one of the best freshman statistical profiles ever logged, and the college-production adjustment ranks him the most NBA-ready rookie. Both project as top-90 players immediately; no other rookie cracks 110.

№ 140 · The steepest fall

Ja Morant, unranked star

Two straight injury-shredded seasons, a −1.5 BPM in 20 games, and a trade to Portland for role players. The model only sees decline; #140 is what the data honestly supports. The Dame-and-Ja backcourt is now the league's most fascinating rehab project.

The Achilles class

Haliburton #56 · Lillard #151 · Kyrie #137

All three missed the entire season. The model rates them on their last healthy year, then applies the tendon tax (×0.78–0.88) and a full-season availability discount. Haliburton's age (25) keeps him in starter territory; Dame's 36th birthday does most of the damage to his.

№ 41 · Trust the data

The Queta problem

Boston's accidental starting center: .674 TS, .224 WS/48, +8.6 filtered on/off across 1,926 minutes. The model says top-45; the eye test says good starter. When every input agrees, the model doesn't blink — this is its most falsifiable claim for 2026-27.

№ 280 · The blind spot

Draymond, honestly mishandled

−2.4 OBPM at 35 and the box score sees almost nothing else he does. A camera-tracking model would rank him 100+ spots higher; a box model can't, and pretending otherwise would be curve-fitting. Same caveat applies to Herb Jones (#312) — though CTG's filtered on/off (−4.3) sides with the box score on Draymond. Flagged, not fixed.

v3 · The subscriber-data pass

What Cleaning the Glass changed

Swapping bbref's raw on/off for CTG's garbage-time-filtered version moved the middle of the board: Maxey → #10 (+7.5 filtered vs +3.4 raw), Franz Wagner → #29, Brunson up three, LaMelo to #11 while Edwards slid to #22 (his flat on-court impact is real, not noise). Biggest casualty: Jalen Williams → #54 — CTG scores his 2024-25 on/off at −12.5, a title-team bench artifact the model can't distinguish from signal. The top seven didn't move.

№ 58 · The correction

Jimmy Butler, moved by a fact-check

v1 of this model ranked Butler #13 on elite per-minute play (5.5 BPM, .646 TS, +11.6 on/off in 38 games) — because it didn't know why the season stopped. He tore his right ACL on Jan 19, 2026 and is expected back around February 2027, at age 37. With the same injury discount applied to every other rehab case, he lands #58. The model only fixes what it can see; this one needed the news.

№ 354 · Sunk cost

Bradley Beal is done being ranked on reputation

Six games, a hip surgery, age 32, and a mediocre last healthy season. The model puts him below 40 rookies and two-way wings on expected contribution — brutal, and probably right within ±50 spots.

Boundary decisions

  • Included: all 584 players with 2025-26 NBA minutes (minus retirees), four contracted stars who missed the season injured (Haliburton, Lillard, Irving, VanVleet), and all 60 picks of the 2026 draft. Teams reflect trades through July 7, 2026 (Giannis→MIA, Kawhi→TOR, Brown↔George, LaMelo→MIN, Morant→POR, Kessler→LAL, etc.).
  • Excluded: 2025-26 retirees (Chris Paul, Kyle Lowry, P.J. Tucker, Gallinari), and unsigned free agents who sat out 2025-26 entirely (Ben Simmons, Malik Beasley, Terry Rozier).

Known limitations — read before arguing

  • Box-score models undervalue tracking-invisible defense (Draymond, Herb Jones) and screen/connective value. v3 integrates Cleaning the Glass's garbage-time-filtered on/off for both seasons (matched 278 of 279 players with 1,000+ minutes; bbref raw as fallback below that).
  • Rookie placements are priors, not measurements. Expect ±60 spots of error by February.
  • Availability & role discounts are choices, documented above. A pure talent ranking would move Giannis to #3, Embiid up ~15 spots, and Queta down ~15.
